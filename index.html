"This artifact shows that the model generates more than nature ever built — which makes evolution's choices visible as choices."

In 1966, paleontologist David M. Raup used three geometric parameters to describe every coiled shell that could theoretically exist. He then mapped where actual species — ammonites, gastropods, bivalves, brachiopods — fall within that space. The striking result: most of the mathematically possible forms are empty. No organism has ever built them.

This is the digital double working in reverse. Instead of modeling something that exists, the model defines the entire neighborhood of existence, and nature's actual solutions appear as a small, selective cluster within a vast geometric silence. Drag the sliders into the gray region of the morphospace map to visit shells the biosphere never made — geometrically coherent, biologically absent.
